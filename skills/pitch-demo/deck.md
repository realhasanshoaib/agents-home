# The proposal deck

The deck makes one argument, in the order the buyer decides: what changes for them, the proof, what is real and what is proposed, and the one next step. Its words come from the brief, so a builder lays them out and doesn't write new claims; a gap goes back to the lead. Legend's deck, `/Users/macmini/code/legend-autohub-demo/public/proposal/index.html`, is a visual shell to reuse (brands, full bleed, embeds), not a running order.

## Slides

These are the sections to choose from, in their usual order. The lead picks only those the buyer's next decision needs, within 6 slides for a cold prospect and 9 for a warm one. The first and last are always there.

| Section | Brand |
| --- | --- |
| What changes for them, in one sentence | Both |
| Their pain, in their words or our proof, with the source | Theirs |
| The demo, opened at the hard moment from the brief | Theirs |
| What works today and what we would build | Theirs |
| A project that proves we can handle this buyer's specific risk | Ours |
| Scope and the time from signing to go-live | Theirs |
| The offer | Ours |
| What we need from you, only once they've agreed to build | Theirs |
| The next step | Both |

## Look

- **Two brands.** Their slides use their colours, font and logo, and ours use Q9's paper and ink. Every slide carries a logo pair at the foot: "<Prospect> × Q9 Labs" on theirs and "Q9 Labs × <Prospect>" on ours. There is no light and dark switch, because each slide takes its brand's colours.
- **Full bleed.** Each slide fills the window at any shape, with no bars above, below or beside it.
- **Visuals over text.** Every screenshot shows the current product while it works, never a test screen or a marketing home page. Add an icon, a photo of their real products or our agent's face only when it helps explain the proof. Group things with spacing and soft filled panels. Hasan dislikes thin lines between rows and accent lines over columns.
- **Whole pictures.** Show every photo at its natural proportions, never cut to fill a fixed strip. On a phone, the first screen shows the promise before any photo.

## Demos on the slides

Each demo plays on its slide and loads only when its slide comes up. Make the proof big enough to read at 390 px: show the moment that matters at full width, and keep frames for when the frame itself helps. Next to each demo, a labelled button such as "Open the live site" opens it alone, and one plain line says it is live and can be clicked. A small corner icon alone is too easy to miss. Every picture of the product shows the current build.

## Proof

Show a past project only when it proves we can handle this buyer's specific risk, with a real screenshot and an outcome, and say whether it was a demo or is live. Edli (LMS-School), our main traction product, is the first candidate for a school. Name clients only as Q9 Brain Fact `q9-deck-client-naming` allows, and describe the rest by industry and country.

## Commercials and the next step

- **The offer.** Show only the offer in the brief: the setup fee, paid in full upfront on signing and never split, the monthly fee and what it includes, usage above that, and the delivery time, under one "Tentative, depends on the final scope" note. Leave out the first-year total. When the full build isn't scoped, show the brief's bounded first phase instead.
- **The next step.** The last slide asks for the brief's one dated next step, the same one the email asks for, with a working reply link to the relationship owner. Show both logos. Hasan's name and photo stay off it.

## Before it goes

The deck is unlisted, with `noindex`, a preview card, and a print layout so they can save it as a PDF. Done when the contact sheets from the pre-send check show every slide readable at 1440 × 900 and 390 px with every picture whole, the proof controls and the print layout work, and one live call started from inside the deck on a phone has run. Check another window size only to chase a problem you've seen.
