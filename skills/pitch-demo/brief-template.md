# Brief: <prospect>

The lead fills in every field, citing `research.md` for each fact. Write "unknown" rather than guess; an unknown becomes a question for the meeting, not a claim. Builders use these words as written.

## The buyer

- **Who decides, and who reads it first:** names and roles, with sources.
- **The decision they face next:** the one thing still unresolved for them, such as whether the idea works in their trade, whether it's feasible with their systems, or what to take to management.
- **What they fear:** in their words where they said it, otherwise marked as our assumption.
- **What they already have:** the services, channels and tools they run today, so we don't pitch what exists.

## The pain

Strongest first, each as one sentence they can check, with its source.

## The proof moment

The single moment in the demo that answers their decision, described as the visitor sees it. This is the hard case, not the intake.

## The offer (only after a meeting where price came up)

Setup fee (paid in full on signing), monthly fee, what the monthly fee includes, usage above that, and the delivery time from signing to go-live. If the full build isn't scoped yet, offer a bounded first phase instead of inventing a price or a date.

## The deck's words

Slide by slide, in order, only the sections this buyer needs (see `deck.md`): the headline, the supporting lines, and what the visual shows.

## The video plan (only when a video was asked for)

The opening outcome in the first five seconds, the proof shots in order, the spoken and caption words, the ending, and the errors that disqualify a take.

## The email

Subject, and the body in the owner's voice, carrying the deck link and the ask.
