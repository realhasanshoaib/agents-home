# What a 9 looks like

The lead writes the brief to this page, and the builders check their work against it. Each area is scored from 1 to 10 as the prospect's decision maker would on first open, and a pitch needs 9 or more in every area that applies; the video doesn't apply when none was asked for. Each area lists what a 9 needs, then what the October 2026 audit found keeps a pitch at 4 or 5.

## 1. Research and targeting

A 9 shows that someone studied this business. Every fact about them is true and has a source. The pain is theirs, in their words or their numbers, and the pitch checks what they already offer before it claims a gap. At least one point is something only a person who studied them would know, and the central problem depends on their circumstances, not only their name. A baseline we don't know becomes a question for the meeting, never an invented loss or saving.

Held back by: Axon was pitched 24/7 answers it already offers; OnTime's agent recommended RAKEZ for a Dubai free-zone question; GCAA's sample proposed a national drone registry that already exists; KU's applicant names didn't fit KU.

## 2. Story and content

A 9 tells the decision maker on the first screen, in one sentence, what changes for them. Every slide answers one of four questions: why this buyer, what proof, what is real and what is only proposed, and what one decision comes next. Cut any slide that answers none. The copy is plain and specific, with numbers where we have them, and says what the demo does not prove yet.

Held back by: opening with the mechanism instead of the outcome, feature tours, two slides about us before the slide the buyer needs, and demos that leave the buyer to do homework to find the point.

## 3. Call to action

A 9 ends with one small, dated next step that settles the buyer's next unresolved decision: an expert review, a feasibility test, a scope review to take to management, or a pilot with what it must prove. A cold prospect usually gets "15 minutes on Tuesday 28 Oct or Wednesday 29 Oct, reply with a time". The same ask closes the deck and the email, with a working reply link to the relationship owner. Nothing else competes with it.

Held back by: a slogan where the ask belongs, an ask with no date, or several asks at once.

## 4. Design and polish

A 9 is easy to read at 1440 × 900 and at 390 px wide, and the proof is readable at both, not a tiny site inside a browser frame inside a slide. Nothing is clipped, overflowing or stale, the brands are consistent, and nothing looks generated. It passes the `squint` skill.

Held back by: product proof shrunk into frames, a phone picture showing an old screen, product photos ahead of the promise on a phone.

## 5. The live demo

A 9 works when the prospect opens it, on a real phone, and does the hard part the buyer worries about, not only the easy intake. It passes every acceptance case in the brief, including two exceptions an expert in their trade would raise, and keeps blocked what must stay blocked. The deck shows the proof without needing a microphone. When a vendor fails, it says the live call is unavailable and shows the video or a captured result, and never blames the visitor's connection.

Held back by: every voice demo failing because the Ultravox account had no credit; fee checks illustrated instead of shown; a quote drafted while a required rating was still missing.

## 6. The video

A 9 lands the buyer's outcome in the first five seconds, shows the hard case, and stays between 30 and 60 seconds. It is a live take of the real product, with one visible caption layer, simulated steps labelled, and sound checked by ear on a phone. It sells the result; it doesn't just show the screen.

Held back by: openings that show the mechanism, videos over 60 seconds, and illustrated steps passed off as the product.
